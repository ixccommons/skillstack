# Positioning and customer definition

**This module doesn't contain the positioning work. It delegates.**

The positioning flow lives in the `marketing-workshop` skill — eight steps,
about 40 minutes, built on Ries and Trout's method: the category ladder the
prospect files you under, the word the leader already owns, the hole nobody is
standing in, and one word plus one sentence short enough to survive in a
crowded head.

Running a second version of it here would give the same person two positions
and no way to tell which one the landing page was built from. So this module is
the seam: three short steps around forty minutes of someone else's flow, worth
about 45 minutes in total.

Say that out loud at the start. People should know they're being handed to
another flow and coming back, not that the session lost its thread.

---

## Step 1 — Hand over what the base module already decided

Read `company-brief.md` before starting anything, and say what's already there.
Positioning opens by asking about the product and who buys it; a founder who
answered that forty minutes ago should not be asked again.

Map it across:

| From `company-brief.md` | Feeds positioning's |
|---|---|
| Problem | Step 1, the offer in plain words |
| Who it's for | Step 2's ladder, and step 4's hole — as a *working* answer to be sharpened, never as the settled segment |
| Chosen approach | What the product actually is, for step 1 |
| The premises from base step 2 | Step 3's read on what the leader owns, and what nobody has tested |

**Say which of these are working answers rather than findings.** "Who it's for"
came out of a forty-minute idea session, not customer evidence. Positioning's
job is to break it if it's wrong, and it can only do that if it knows the answer
is provisional.

The full contract of what positioning expects as input — fifteen fields, and
what to do when one is missing — is in
`marketing-workshop/references/positioning-input.md`. Read it rather than
guessing at the handover.

**Write to brief:** one line noting the handover and what was carried across.

---

## Step 2 — Run the positioning flow

Run `marketing-workshop`'s positioning flow, start to finish. It is
authoritative for these forty minutes; follow it exactly and don't blend it with
anything from this skill.

Two things stay true across the seam, because they're this session's rules
rather than that flow's: **one step per turn**, and the response posture in
`SKILL.md` — no opening validation, comfort is a signal to keep going, disagree
with the answer and not the person. The positioning flow has its own push-backs
and they're compatible; where the two say the same thing, it isn't a conflict.

**If the positioning flow isn't reachable** — the marketing skill isn't
installed, or this is a pasted chat with no skills directory — don't improvise a
positioning flow from memory. Say plainly that it's a separate flow and give the
person the two ways to get it:

- Install the marketing skill and run `/marketing-workshop`, asking for the
  positioning flow.
- Or open a new chat with
  `marketing-workshop/portable/paste/positioning.md`, run it there, and bring
  the finished brief back here to step 3.

Coming back with a pasted brief is a normal path, not a degraded one — the
positioning flow prints its brief at the end precisely so it can travel.

**Write to brief:** nothing from this step directly. The positioning flow writes
its own brief; step 3 is what brings it home.

---

## Step 3 — Carry it back

Positioning finishes with its own brief. Read it and bring the parts this arc
needs into `company-brief.md`:

| From positioning | Lands in `company-brief.md` |
|---|---|
| Primary segment | **Who it's for** — replacing the working answer from the base module |
| Value proposition (the sentence) | **Value proposition** (header) |
| The one word | **The one word** (header) |
| Category, the ladder, the hole | The module's free-text section |
| Voice rules, objections, the three ranked points | Left in the positioning brief — the copy flow reads them there, and copying them here creates a second source of truth |

**Replacing "Who it's for" is the point of the module**, so make the change
visible rather than silent: note in this module's section what it used to say,
what it says now, and what changed it. A founder who can't remember why their
segment narrowed will widen it again within a month.

**Push back on:** a positioned segment that came back identical to the working
answer.

*They say:* "Yeah, it confirmed what I already thought — small business owners."
*Weak:* "Great, that's a strong signal your instinct was right." — treats an
untested guess surviving as evidence, which is exactly the everybody trap
getting a second life with a rubber stamp.
*Better:* "That's possible, but it's also what happens when the flow gets run
fast. Which group did it make you turn away, and what was the hole? If neither
has an answer, the segment didn't get tested."

**Write to brief:** Who it's for (updated), Value proposition (header), The one
word (header), and the revision note.

---

## Closing the module

Read back the sentence and the one word. Nothing else — both briefs hold the
rest.

Then the checkpoint: tell them to post their one word and their sentence where
the room can see it, and next to it the segment they started the day believing
in. The pair is the interesting part.

**Then what's next.** The landing page comes from the same marketing skill's
copy flow, which reads the positioning brief directly — the value proposition,
the three ranked points, the objections and the voice rules are all already
there. It opens by auditing whatever page exists against the position just
built, and for someone who arrived this morning with an idea, there's no page
yet: that branch is written and it starts by deciding what one action the page
has to produce.

---

## If they already have positioning

Some people arrive with a segment and a value proposition they're confident in,
from earlier work or an earlier session. Don't re-run forty minutes to confirm
it.

Ask for it, write it into the header, and run only the two tests that catch the
common failures: **could a direct competitor say this sentence unchanged?** and
**who does it exclude?** If it survives both, take it and move on to the copy
flow. If it fails either, that's the moment to offer the full flow — now with a
reason, which is a much easier sell than offering it cold.
